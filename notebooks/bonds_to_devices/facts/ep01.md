# Episode 1 fact sheet — Why hydrogen comes in pairs

**One idea:** two hydrogen atoms have less energy together than apart, so they stay paired.

| On screen | Value | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Bond length rₑ | 74.144 pm | Huber & Herzberg (1979) via NIST WebBook; Atkins' Physical Chemistry |
| Well depth Dₑ | 4.7483 eV | Kołos & Wolniewicz (1968); consistent with D₀ plus the zero-point energy (2179 cm⁻¹) |
| Bond energy D₀ | 4.4781 eV (36118.07 cm⁻¹), 432 kJ/mol | Liu et al. (2009); NIST WebBook |
| Vibration ωₑ | 4401.21 cm⁻¹ → 1.32 × 10¹⁴ Hz | Huber & Herzberg (1979); NIST WebBook |
| Pairs per litre | 2.46 × 10²² at 1 atm, 25 °C | Ideal gas law with CODATA 2018 k_B |
| Thermal energy k_BT | 0.0257 eV at 298.15 K | CODATA 2018 |

**Trap checks**

- H₂ is infrared-inactive (no dipole). Its vibration is seen by Raman scattering. Do not say infrared.
- 74 pm is the bottom of the curve; the rotationally measured bond length r₀ is about 75 pm.
- The Morse curve is too deep beyond about 200 pm; do not quote its energy there as measured.
